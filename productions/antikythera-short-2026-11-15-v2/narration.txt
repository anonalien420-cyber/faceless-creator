A geared bronze machine that predicted eclipses existed in the ancient world about two thousand years ago. Sponge divers found the wreck off Antikythera in 1900, recovered the corroded lump in 1901, and its fragments are now held at the National Archaeological Museum in Athens. In 1902 a gear was spotted inside the corroded lump, and the museum's Valerios Stais studied it. What survives is 82 fragments containing 30 bronze gear wheels. The mechanism was built around 100 BCE, give or take thirty years. Nothing comparably complex appears again for about 1,400 years, until the great clocks of medieval cathedrals. What remains unknown: No inscription on the mechanism names its builder, and no ancient text describes the workshop that made it or the person who paid for it. The evidence is what we have. The unanswered questions are what remain. What do you think happened? What does the record actually support?
