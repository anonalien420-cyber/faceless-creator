# Ancient Egyptian sound evidence review package

**Episode candidate:** The Sacred Secrets of SOUND  
**Status:** REVIEW REQUIRED. No source or claim is approved.

## Editorial guardrail

The trending Gaia video is a trend signal, not a factual source. The research below supports studying sound in ancient Egyptian worship spaces, musical practice, and instrument acoustics. It does **not** establish that sound frequencies built the pyramids, that ancient Egyptians possessed a modern quantum theory of sound, or that temple acoustics were deliberately engineered for supernatural effects.

Keep the Dendera temple (Ptolemaic/Roman-era) separate from Old Kingdom pyramid construction.

## Source review queue

### snd_src_001 — Dendera temple acoustic measurement

- **Paper:** “Assessing the Acoustics of an Ancient Egyptian temple,” IEEE, *2021 Immersive and 3D Audio (I3DA)*.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1109/i3da48870.2021.9610973
- **Review anchor:** Abstract; sections II–IV; measurement campaign and post-processing.
- **Candidate relevance:** Reports an acoustic measurement campaign at the Dendera temple using a spherical microphone array and directional room impulse responses.
- **Critical limitation:** The paper says no text has been found showing that an acoustic theory governed the design of Egyptian monuments. Whether the observed properties were intentional or incidental remains a matter of debate.
- **Status:** UNREVIEWED

### snd_src_002 — Instrument volume and venue acoustics

- **Paper:** Heidi Köpp-Junk, “The Sound of Ancient Egypt – Acoustics and Volume Measurements,” *Ars inter Culturas* 9 (2020), pp. 13–33.
- **Original record/full text:** https://cejsh.icm.edu.pl/cejsh/element/bwmeta1.element.desklight-57e2fef5-1f97-4f03-bc8c-a9958538e2e0
- **Review anchor:** Full article, methods and measurements of replica instruments.
- **Candidate relevance:** Discusses acoustic conditions at ancient Egyptian performance venues and measurements of replicas of ancient Egyptian musical instruments.
- **Critical limitation:** Replica measurements and venue comparisons do not establish supernatural effects or hidden engineering technology.
- **Status:** UNREVIEWED

### snd_src_003 — Tell el-Farcha rattles

- **Paper:** Katarzyna Tatoń and Ireneusz Czajka, “The Rattles from Tell el-Farcha: Acoustic Research with the Use of Numerical Sound Reconstruction,” *Studies in Ancient Art and Civilization* 25 (2021), pp. 7–29.
- **Original PDF:** https://journals.akademicka.pl/saac/article/download/4541/3909/5901
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.12797/SAAC.25.2021.25.01
- **Review anchor:** Full paper, acoustic analysis and numerical reconstruction.
- **Candidate relevance:** Uses acoustic analysis and numerical reconstruction to study ancient Egyptian clay rattles and explore aspects of past soundscapes.
- **Critical limitation:** A reconstruction is a research model, not a recording of the exact sound heard in antiquity.
- **Status:** UNREVIEWED

## Candidate claim worksheet

1. **snd_claim_001 — KNOWN candidate.** A 2021 study measured acoustic properties in the Dendera temple using a spherical microphone array and room-impulse-response analysis. Source: snd_src_001.
2. **snd_claim_002 — DEBATED candidate.** The Dendera study says there is no known ancient Egyptian text demonstrating that a formal acoustic theory governed temple design; intentional design versus incidental acoustics remains debated. Source: snd_src_001.
