## Executive finding

The package is substantially grounded in real, relevant primary or institutional sources. Five claims are supportable with careful wording, one is an interpretive claim that must remain DEBATED, and the seventh source is suitable for baseline context only. The episode title's “oldest cover-up” framing is unsupported and should be replaced before scripting.

## Source-by-source review

### gp_src_001 — ScanPyramids Big Void
**Recommendation: APPROVE FOR HUMAN REVIEW (high confidence).**

Nature's 2017 paper reports a large void above the Grand Gallery, with a minimum length of 30 metres. It says the detection was first made with nuclear emulsion films, then confirmed with scintillator hodoscopes and gas detectors, with three independent analyses. The authors explicitly state that the void's intended purpose is unknown.

**Safe claim:** “Muon imaging detected a large void above the Grand Gallery, at least 30 metres long. Three different detection technologies independently confirmed the signal.”

**Do not say:** hidden room, secret chamber, tomb, treasure chamber, or that archaeologists know what it was for.

**Review anchors:** Nature 552 (2017), pp. 386–390; DOI 10.1038/nature24647; abstract and full article. Nature's page was indexed but its direct page fetch redirected to an inaccessible identity gateway during this audit; the abstract was cross-checked through the DOI record and IAEA INIS bibliographic record.

### gp_src_002 — North Face Corridor
**Recommendation: APPROVE FOR HUMAN REVIEW (high confidence).**

The 2023 Nature Communications paper reports a corridor-shaped void behind the North Face Chevron. The two detector teams' estimates are compatible; the paper gives dimensions of approximately 2.02 m wide, 2.18 m high, and 9.06 m long. The paper does not establish its original function. It also says the North Face Corridor does not appear to connect to the ScanPyramids Big Void, although a smaller connecting passage cannot be completely ruled out.

**Safe claim:** “A separate corridor-shaped void behind the North Face Chevron was measured at about 9 metres long and roughly 2 metres by 2 metres in cross-section.”

**Guardrail:** Keep it distinct from the 2017 Big Void. Do not imply that its function is known or that the two voids connect.

**Review anchors:** Nature Communications 14, 1144 (2 March 2023), abstract, Results, Discussion, Table 1; DOI 10.1038/s41467-023-36351-0. Direct Nature fetch was blocked by the publisher's identity gateway; indexed article text and PubMed metadata were available.

### gp_src_003 — Merer logbook / Wadi el-Jarf papyri
**Recommendation: APPROVE FOR HUMAN REVIEW (high confidence, with wording restrictions).**

The published English translation in Tallet's IFAO volume directly records Inspector Merer's team hauling/loading limestone at Tura and travelling with stone toward Akhet-Khufu. The editor's summary explains that the logbooks document river transport of fine limestone from Tura to the Great Pyramid construction site. The same summary explicitly says the papyri do not describe the pyramid's construction techniques.

**Safe claim:** “Merer's logbook records a work team transporting limestone by river from Tura toward the Great Pyramid site while the monument was under construction.”

**Guardrails:** Identify the specific record as Papyrus A/B and the relevant section/day when used in narration. Preserve fragmentary brackets and question marks in the translation. Do not claim the logbook explains how the pyramid was engineered, how every block was moved, or the full workforce.

**Review anchors:** Tallet, *Les papyrus de la mer Rouge I: Le journal de Merer* (IFAO, MIFAO 136, 2017), English translation, Papyrus B sections BI–BIV, printed pp. 150–151; summary and conclusion, printed pp. 151–153 and 160. The English translation is in the published volume's Annex I.

### gp_src_004 — Heit el-Ghurab / Lost City
**Recommendation: APPROVE FOR HUMAN REVIEW (high confidence, qualification mandatory).**

AERA's project page describes a 4th Dynasty settlement extending over more than 7 hectares, with workshops, industrial yards, bakeries, commissaries, kitchens, warehouses, houses, and administrative spaces. AERA describes it as a base for the pyramid complexes of Menkaure, Khafre, and probably Khufu.

**Safe claim:** “Heit el-Ghurab was a large 4th Dynasty settlement and base of operations for the Giza pyramid complexes; AERA includes Khufu with an explicit ‘probably’ qualification.”

**Guardrails:** Do not say all residents built Khufu's pyramid, or present the Khufu link as certain. The strongest site-specific claim is the settlement's role across the Giza pyramid complexes, not a simplistic workers' barracks story.

**Review anchors:** AERA, “The Lost City of the Pyramids (Heit el-Ghurab),” project overview, especially settlement extent and functions.

### gp_src_005 — Second boat-pit cover-stone graffiti
**Recommendation: KEEP AS DEBATED / INTERPRETIVE; do not use as a settled fact.**

The J-STAGE abstract confirms graffiti in red, yellow, and black inks on some cover stones. The authors divide markings into at least two stages based on surface treatment and interpret signs and destination marks as indicating different work groups or transport stages. Their abstract repeatedly uses qualified language (“seem,” “presumed,” “suggest”), so the packet's caution is correct.

**Safe claim:** “A 2016 study interprets the graffiti as evidence for at least two stages of stone handling and possibly distinct transport organizations.”

**Guardrails:** Attribute the interpretation to Takahashi and Nishisaka. Do not state that the graffiti conclusively proves two organizations, or treat every sign's meaning as certain.

**Review anchors:** Takahashi & Nishisaka, *Bulletin of the Society for Near Eastern Studies in Japan* 59(1) (2016), pp. 2–13, DOI 10.5356/jorient.59.1_2. The abstract was inspected; a full-PDF close reading of the photographs and argument remains advisable before using specific sign translations.

### gp_src_006 — Great Pyramid Scanning Project / bedrock features
**Recommendation: APPROVE FOR HUMAN REVIEW (high confidence, scope correction).**

AERA's 2023–2024 annual report describes systematic recording of postholes, lever sockets, quarry channels, and other marks in the bedrock around Khufu's complex. It reports thousands of features across more than 70,000 m² and a 2024 scan that recorded 432 archaeological features across nearly 6,000 m² of newly exposed southern bedrock. This is an ongoing documentation project, not a final reconstruction of the entire construction method.

**Safe claim:** “AERA's survey documents thousands of bedrock features around Khufu's complex, including postholes, lever sockets, and quarry channels, which offer clues about construction operations.”

**Guardrails:** Do not claim the features reveal a complete or settled building method. Distinguish the previously surveyed three sides from the newly exposed southern area and its 432 recorded features.

**Citation correction:** The packet's “printed report page 7” points to the preceding Menkaure Valley Temple section. The Great Pyramid Scanning Project begins on printed page 8 and continues on page 9 of the AERA Annual Report 2023–2024. Cite pp. 8–9, especially the text and map/scan descriptions.

### gp_src_007 — Egyptian Ministry monument baseline
**Recommendation: APPROVE FOR HUMAN REVIEW (context only).**

The Ministry of Tourism and Antiquities page identifies the monument as Khufu's tomb, gives the original height as 146.5 metres, and states an estimated 10–20 year construction duration. Crucially, it says the exact construction method is not entirely certain. It describes three chambers and the Grand Gallery.

**Safe use:** Basic monument context only. Label the construction duration as an estimate, not a documented fact.

**Guardrails:** This institutional overview is not a substitute for specialist archaeological research on debated technical details.

**Review anchors:** Egyptian Ministry of Tourism and Antiquities, “The Great Pyramid,” monument overview.

## Editorial and production corrections

1. Replace “The Oldest Cover-Up In History.” No reviewed source supports a cover-up. Suggested factual title: **“The Great Pyramid's Hidden Voids: What Modern Scans Actually Found.”**
2. Build the hook around the confirmed contrast: particle physics detected a void at least 30 metres long, but its purpose remains unknown. This is dramatic without inventing a conspiracy.
3. Keep the 2017 Big Void and 2023 North Face Corridor visually and verbally distinct.
4. Use the Merer papyrus as direct evidence of organized stone transport, not as an answer to the ramp/hoisting question.
5. Present Heit el-Ghurab as a complex settlement and operations base, with Khufu's role qualified.
6. Keep the graffiti interpretation in a clearly attributed “possible interpretation” segment.
7. Cite the AERA Scanning Project at printed pp. 8–9, not p. 7.
8. Do not set evidence-ledger status to APPROVED or `include_in_script: true` on the basis of this AI audit. A designated human reviewer must make those state changes after inspecting the packet and source anchors.
